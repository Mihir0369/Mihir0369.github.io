Half of Toronto's parks and forestry requests are recorded as open: what is 311 telling the city?

When Toronto residents see a problem, such as a missed garbage pickup, a fallen tree or a pothole, they report it to the city by calling 311. I studied 1.35 million of these reports from 2023 to 2025 to find out which kinds of requests are recorded as open at the data snapshot, and whether the rise in reports means more problems or just a change in how they are labelled.

Bottom line: one division leaves about half its requests open, and request type names changed in 2025, so the 14.47% rise in demand cannot be split into more problems and new labels. The extract has no close date, so open means open at extraction, not a confirmed backlog, and time to close cannot be measured.

Key findings

  1. Half of parks and forestry requests are recorded as open: 49.45% in 2025, against 1.37% to 6.60% in every other division.
  2. Demand grew 14.47% in 2025, to 500,269 requests, but request type names changed that year, so growth by type is not comparable.
  3. 58.17% of 2025 requests sit under renamed or new request types, so year-over-year growth by type is not valid.
  4. Dead-wildlife pick-up is cancelled 38.84% of the time, the highest of any large request type.

Data coverage

Source organizationCity of Toronto Open Data: 311 Service Requests, Customer Initiated
Covered period2023 to 2025 (data through December 2025)
GrainOne row per service request: 1,351,594 requests, 7 divisions, 829 request types
DenominatorRequests in the selected year. Open rate = requests with status Open or In Progress divided by all requests
Exclusions and repairsRows damaged in the source CSV were repaired during cleaning; the data-quality page lists them
Known limitationsNo close date, so open means open at the data snapshot, not a confirmed backlog. Request-type names changed in January 2025, so growth by type is not comparable across the break

Limits of this analysis

  • No close date: I report request status as of the data snapshot, not how long anything took.
  • An open status could mean a genuine backlog or a status never updated. The data cannot separate the two.
  • Request type names changed in January 2025, so type-level comparisons are valid only within 2023 to 2024 or within 2025.
  • Ward counts are not adjusted for population, and requests reflect who reports as well as where problems occur.
  • 1.5% of requests have an Unknown status and are excluded from the resolved share.

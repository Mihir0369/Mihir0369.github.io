Opioid deaths declined faster than EMS responses in the reported data: what is Canada's data telling us?

Hospitals, ambulance services and death records all count opioid and stimulant harms, but not in the same way or in the same places. I used the Public Health Agency of Canada's open data, 2016 to early 2026, to see which measures are falling, where the burden is greatest, and what each number really covers.

Bottom line: in the reported annual Canada totals (2023 peak to 2025), deaths fell faster than hospital, emergency and ambulance activity, so deaths alone may understate the burden. Provinces differ several times over, and several Canada totals leave places out.

Recommended business actionTrack EMS and emergency visits next to deaths. From 2023 to 2025, deaths fell 29.43% but EMS responses only 7.84% (annual Canada totals). Deaths alone may understate the ongoing burden, so report all four measures together, noting which places each source covers.

Key findings

  1. Opioid deaths jumped from 3,602 in 2019 to 6,184 in 2020, peaked at 8,083 in 2023 and were 5,704 in 2025, down 29.43%.
  2. Comparing 2025 with the 2023 peak in annual Canada totals, hospital stays are down 21.75%, emergency department visits 28.51% and EMS responses 7.84% (42,756 to 39,406). Deaths fell faster than EMS responses. Coverage differs by source, and 2026 is a partial year.
  3. In 2024 the opioid death rate was 35.7 per 100,000 in British Columbia, 24.4 in Alberta, 14.0 in Ontario and 3.4 in Quebec.
  4. About 71.64% of opioid deaths in 2024 were men, and 52.58% were aged 30 to 49.

Data coverage

Source organizationPublic Health Agency of Canada, Opioid- and Stimulant-related Harms in Canada, Health Infobase (accessed November 2026)
Covered period2016 to Q1 2026, yearly and quarterly. 2026 is a partial year
GrainAggregated counts and rates by province or territory, year and quarter, for four sources: deaths, hospital stays, emergency department visits and EMS responses
DenominatorCounts, with crude rates per 100,000 that are not age-adjusted
Exclusions and coverageSmall counts are suppressed. Hospital stays leave out Quebec, emergency department visits cover 7 of 13 provinces and territories, and a Canada total only adds up the places that report
Known limitationsProvinces report differently, and some 2025 provincial values look provisional, so provinces are compared on 2024

Limits of this analysis

  • Provinces report differently, so rankings are a prompt for questions, not a verdict.
  • Rates are crude, not age-adjusted, and counts depend on population size.
  • A Canada total only adds up the places that report: hospital stays leave out Quebec and emergency department visits cover 7 of 13 provinces and territories.
  • Some 2025 provincial values look provisional (Ontario deaths fell 37.49% and Quebec rose 93.14% in a year), so the report compares provinces on 2024.
  • Small counts are suppressed, so age and sex cells in small regions are blank.
  • No patient-level data, so no conclusions about individuals.

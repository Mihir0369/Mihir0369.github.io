Among recorded fatal and injury collisions, 3.41% were fatal overnight against 1.52% by day: when and where do Canadian roads turn deadly?

Police report every fatal and injury collision in Canada, and Transport Canada publishes the records. I used the 2019 and 2020 files to see when fatal collisions concentrate, which conditions and people show the highest fatal share or fatality proportion among recorded collisions, and what changed when traffic fell in 2020.

Bottom line: fatal collisions concentrate at night, in single-vehicle collisions, on curved roads and where nothing controls traffic, and 2020's drop was almost all injury collisions, so the fatal share rose. These are associations in police-reported data, not causes.

Executive summary

Business problemWhen, where and under what conditions do fatal collisions concentrate, and in which groups the fatality proportion is highest among recorded collisions, so a road-safety analytics team knows what to monitor and what to say about safe driving?
Dataset and scopeTransport Canada, National Collision Database, 2019 and 2020 (real, Open Government Licence). 185,772 collisions, 330,576 vehicles and 463,630 people.
My contributionEnd to end: found the hidden collision ID, checked the data in SQL and Python, built the star-schema model and DAX, designed the seven-page report and wrote the findings
Key finding2.24× higher fatal share overnight (3.41%) than by day (1.52%), among recorded fatal and injury collisions. The denominator is recorded collisions, not traffic volume, so this is not a per-trip risk
Recommended business actionMonitor the fatal share, not only the count. In 2020 collisions fell 24.38% but fatal ones only 1.29%. A falling count can hide a rising share of serious outcomes.
LimitationsNot claims data: it supports no loss ratio, premium or pricing statement. Police-reported fatal and injury collisions only, so shares are of serious collisions.

Key findings

  1. Collisions fell 24.38% in 2020, but fatal collisions only 1.29%, so the fatal share rose from 1.53% to 2.00%. Injury collisions account for 99.92% of the decline.
  2. Among recorded fatal and injury collisions, the fatal share was 3.41% overnight (10 pm to 6 am) versus 1.52% during the day, 2.24 times as high. The denominator is recorded collisions with no traffic volumes, so it is not the chance of a fatal crash per trip. The highest hour is 4 am, at 4.89%.
  3. Curved roads (3.63%), night (3.41%) and single-vehicle collisions (2.73%) have the highest fatal shares. No traffic control carries 2,349 of the 3,225 fatal collisions (72.84%).
  4. Among occupants in recorded collisions, 6.98% of those with no safety device used died against 0.43% with one (16.09 times the proportion). This is an unadjusted, observational comparison: records with unknown device use (21.99%) are left out, and it does not show that the device caused the difference. The gap holds in every age band.

Data coverage

Source organizationTransport Canada, National Collision Database (accessed November 2026)
Covered periodCalendar years 2019 and 2020
GrainPerson-level rows grouped into collisions: 185,772 collisions, 330,576 vehicles and 463,630 people
DenominatorRecorded fatal and injury collisions. There are no traffic volumes or kilometres driven, so shares are not per-trip risk
Exclusions7,416 dummy rows removed. Property-damage-only collisions are not in the file, and there are no insurance claims
Known limitations2020 was a pandemic year. Safety-device use is unknown for 21.99% of records, and unknown means left blank by police or not supplied by that jurisdiction

Limits of this analysis

  • Not claims data: it supports no loss ratio, premium or pricing statement.
  • Police-reported fatal and injury collisions only, so shares are of serious collisions.
  • No traffic volumes or kilometres driven, so nothing here is a risk per trip.
  • Unadjusted associations, not causes. The what-if is an illustrative scenario: it assumes the road causes the gap, so it shows an assumed effect, not an observed outcome.
  • 2020 was a pandemic year, so it is not a typical comparison.
  • Safety-device use is as recorded by police, and 21.99% of records are unknown.

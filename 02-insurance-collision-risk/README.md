# Project 2: Collision severity and risk factors (Transport Canada National Collision Database)

**Role targeted:** Reporting / BI Analyst at a Canadian P&C insurer (underwriting or claims analytics)
**Data:** Transport Canada [National Collision Database](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/1eb9eba7-71d1-4b30-9fb1-30cbdab7e63a), police-reported fatal and injury collisions for 2019 and 2020 (Open Government Licence - Canada). The [data dictionary](https://open.canada.ca/data/dataset/1eb9eba7-71d1-4b30-9fb1-30cbdab7e63a/resource/21eb7966-38da-4814-a80e-521bce6c4c27/download/data_dictionary.pdf) was used to check every code label.

## Business question
When, where and under what conditions do fatal collisions concentrate, and who is most at risk, so an insurer's analytics team knows what to monitor and what to say about safe driving?

## What this data is and is not
- Police-reported **fatal and injury** collisions only. No property-damage-only collisions.
- Not insurance claims. The report does not show loss ratios, premiums or pricing.
- One row per person in each vehicle: 185,772 collisions, 330,576 vehicles and 463,630 people after cleaning.
- 2020 is a pandemic year, so it is not a typical comparison.

## Results
| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| Collisions fell in 2020 | 105,782 to 79,990 (-24.4%) |
| Fatal collisions fell much less | 1,623 to 1,602 (-1.3%), so the fatal share rose from 1.5% to 2.0% |
| Where the decline came from | injury collisions: 99.9% of the 25,792 fewer collisions |
| Night against day fatal share | 3.4% against 1.5%; riskiest hour 4 am (4.9%) |
| Highest fatal share by condition | curved roads 3.6%, night 3.4%, single-vehicle collisions 2.7%, no traffic control 2.4% (signals 0.6%) |
| Where most deaths happen | no traffic control: 2,349 of 3,225 fatal collisions (73%) |
| Occupants, no safety device used | 7.0% fatality rate against 0.43% with one (16 times); the gap holds in every age band |
| Road users | motorcyclists 3.7% and pedestrians 2.9% against drivers 0.57% |

These are unadjusted associations in police-reported data, not causal effects.

## How to rebuild it
1. `python load_ncdb.py` downloads the two CSVs into `data/`, validates the columns, cleans, and writes `ncdb.db` (tables `collisions`, `vehicles`, `persons`) with printed checks.
2. `python build_star.py` writes the star schema to `model/`: two fact tables and 15 dimensions.
3. Run the queries in `sql/analysis.sql` (every number above is reproduced there).
4. Open `powerbi/collision-risk.pbip` in Power BI Desktop (set the `ModelFolder` parameter to the `model/` folder).
5. The report and model are generated by `build_powerbi.py` (TMDL and PBIR text files, so they are versionable).

## Data traps handled (all checked in the load printout)
- **Collision ID:** the file has a key, `C_CASE`, that the data dictionary does not mention. It is unique, contiguous, and the severity, hour, weather and road surface never change within a case. An ID derived from "vehicle 1, person 1" splits and merges collisions, so `C_CASE` is used.
- **Code formats differ between years** ("2" in 2019, "02" in 2020), so codes are cast to numbers before mapping.
- **Unknown values use letter codes** (U, UU, Q, QQ, X, XX, *, **). They are kept as "Unknown or not reported" and never counted as a real category or zero.
- **Dummy rows:** a parked car gets a dummy person and a pedestrian gets a dummy vehicle (7,416 rows). These are not people or vehicles, so they are excluded.
- **Two collisions have no month,** so they drop out of month-level views; the full-year cards and tables include them and the footnotes say so.

## Data model
Star schema. `fact_collision` (one row per collision) and `fact_person` (one row per person) with 15 dimensions. Every dimension has an "unknown" member (key 0), so no row disappears from a chart as (Blank). A `Date` table, a `Time Comparison` calculation group (current, prior year, change, % change), a `Scenario` table and an `Uptake` what-if table support the report. 99 DAX measures live in a `_Measures` table.

## Report pages
1. **Start here:** the question, four headline numbers, what the data shows and what to do about it.
2. **When:** fatal share by hour and weekday (conditionally formatted heat map) and by hour (night bars highlighted).
3. **Compare:** 2020 against 2019 with time intelligence, fatal against injury indexed to 2019, and a breakdown of where the decline came from.
4. **Conditions:** a what-if scenario. A parameter sets how much of the gap between risky and safer roads to close, and the page shows the fatal collisions that could be avoided.
5. **People:** fatality rate by road user, age, safety device and vehicle, relative to the average person.
6. **Explore:** a decomposition tree to split fatal collisions by any factor.
7. **Data quality:** unknown rates by field, what was excluded, and plain-language terms.

## Limits
- No traffic volumes or kilometres driven, so nothing here is a risk per trip.
- Police-reported only, and safety-device use is as recorded by police.
- The what-if assumes the gap between conditions is caused by the road, which the data cannot prove; the levers overlap and cannot be added.
